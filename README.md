# Alejandro Garbi

I am a fullstack engineer in Calgary. I have spent about ten years designing, shipping, and keeping web products alive — APIs, UIs, data models, and the cloud around them.

I care more about whether a system is understandable six months later than whether it was clever on day one. Types, explicit domain models, indexes that match real queries, and a deployment path you can trust tend to matter more than the framework of the week. I like work where frontend and backend are the same problem: a screen that has to be honest about the data, and an API that has to stay honest under load.

I trained as a computer engineer at Universidad Simón Bolívar in Venezuela. Early on I was a fullstack developer and then a team lead at TransaMovil, where we built a virtual wallet and e-commerce product (Node.js, Sails, Angular, PostgreSQL, payments). That job taught me to own a product end to end, including the unglamorous parts: requirements, sprints, production deploys, and a small team.

I later spent several years at BairesDev on Vans Customs 3D, a 3D footwear, backpack, and apparel design platform. I started in production support (Sev1–Sev3 incidents, AWS, releases) and moved into senior backend work: Node/TypeScript, MySQL, Redis, Terraform, and leading two engineers while still writing code. That stretch is where I got serious about incidents, 3D on the web, and shipping in someone else’s production.

I now work in Calgary on product engineering in TypeScript: federated GraphQL, Node.js resolvers, React, MongoDB, Redis, and AWS with Terraform. I will not name the employer here. The through-line is the same: make the graph and the data model boringly correct, then keep the apps on top of it from falling over.

Outside of paid work I still like taking a technical idea and making it visible. [3D Brain Anatomy](https://github.com/alexgarbi10/threejs) is a Three.js sketch of labeled brain regions (WebGL, CSS2D labels, Vite). It is a small public repo, not a product, but it sits on the same curiosity as the 3D commerce work: can you put a spatial model in a browser and make it readable?

I work in Spanish (native) and English (professional). I am straightforward in reviews and in incidents. I would rather say “I do not know yet” than paper over a gap.
